  1. 1The sheet is the editor. Edit turns the Changes sheet's text into fields: the title, subtitle, key and composer are edited where they print. While editing, the sheet shows concert pitch and turns guide tones off, so what you type is what the chart stores.
  2. 2Click a chord, type. It opens as a small field on its beat with the chord's text selected; completions come from the chord qualities the app knows, each with the scale it would get. Tab moves to the next bar, Space adds a second chord at beat 3.
  3. 3Live result. Each completion shows the scale it would get, and the numeral and scale under the chord update as you type (the analysis runs on each change), so “is it V7 or V7/iv?” is answered where you look.
  4. 4Bar toolbar on hover or focus: insert before or after, split, delete, ⋯ (key change, endings, segno and coda, new section). Engine edits renumber what follows.
  5. 5Scales and numerals are pickers. The dotted labels that today open “Why” also open the scale list and the function list.
  6. 6A ghost “+ bar” closes every section; “+ section” follows the last line.
  7. Hard parts. Positions come from VexFlow's drawing (xs per line), so fields wait for a redraw; a written-out @copy (“A3 (= A1)”) or a repeat (“A1 · A2”) edits its source, which has to be said; a phone line holds two bars, and its targets are small; printing must drop every editing mark.
