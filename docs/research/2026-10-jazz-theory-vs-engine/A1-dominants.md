## 0. Answers to the specific adjudication questions

**(a) Is "tensions diatonic to the key of the moment" (principle 4, §7.1) Berklee's actual rule?**
Yes for secondary dominants with diatonic roots; it is stated almost in the engine's words, but it carries
four documented exceptions, and it is not Berklee's rule for dominants with nondiatonic roots.

- N&G p. 43: "The chord scales for secondary dominants reflect these common characteristics: chord tones and
  diatonic non-chord tones." M&H p. 42 (boxed): "Chord scales for secondary dominants consist of chord tones
  and tensions diatonic to the key."
- N&G p. 92 (minor): "if the root of a chord is diatonic, think diatonic for the chord scale construction; if
  the root of a chord is nondiatonic, the construction of the chord scale needs a logical justification."
  Dominants "which have expected resolutions down a perfect fifth use some form of Mixolydian chord scale";
  those "other than down a perfect fifth use a Lydian♭7 chord scale."
- Exception 1, the ♯9 rider. N&G p. 43: "If ♭9 is available, ♯9 is also available … These scales will contain
  8 different pitches." M&H p. 44 (E7 in C): ♭9, ♯9, ♭13; p. 95 (V7 of minor): "Mixolydian (♭9, ♯9, ♭13)".
  So wherever the key supplies the ♭9, Berklee's scale is the 8-note one (the "Spanish Phrygian" of the user's
  convention); the engine's Phrygian Dominant is its 7-note subset.
- Exception 2, altered stays altered. N&G p. 45: "Altered notes should not be changed to unaltered as this
  action would change the resolution expectation."
- Exception 3, target quality outranks the key. M&H p. 44: "Mixolydian when resolving to a target with a major 3 /
  Mixolydian with ♭13 when resolving to a target with a minor 3". M&H p. 45: the diatonic scale for V7/II
  (A7 in C: 9, ♭13) "has issues" (mixed major and minor tensions; the 9 is the leading tone; a second tritone).
  M&H p. 46: "Since the advent of the bebop style (post 1945), it has been more common to choose the diatonic ♯9
  as the partner for ♭13", giving A7 (♭9, ♯9, ♭13). In minor, M&H p. 100: the natural-minor default for V7/IV
  "would be Mixolydian (♭13). However, the target of V7/IV is a minor chord. In common practice, it shares the
  same set of tensions as V7 and V7/V: Mixolydian (♭9, ♯9, ♭13)"; summary table p. 103 ("Mixolydian (♭13) but
  *better* the optional Mixolydian (♭9, ♯9, ♭13)").
- Exception 4, the minor key's reference is wider than the engine's. N&G p. 91–92 and M&H p. 94–95 both reject
  one fixed minor scale and use the composite (natural plus raised 6th and 7th; M&H: natural minor "with
  leading tone"). The engine's principle 5 uses the same word "composite" for something narrower: natural
  minor, with harmonic minor only for the primary V7.
- Where Berklee uses the chord's own scale instead of the key: subV7 (M&H p. 65: diatonic tensions give
  "inconsistent and sometimes painful results"; N&G p. 57); sequential dominants (N&G p. 51: "The chord scales
  do not have a diatonic orientation"); special-function and non-resolving dominants (N&G p. 123, 127; M&H
  p. 238); blues (N&G p. 105; M&H p. 133–135: "no single modal source that defines blues tonality").
- Levine does not use this rule at all. His assignments are by chord type and resolution: Mixolydian on V7
  (p. 36); alt on V7 in a minor II–V–I (p. 75); half-whole on V7♭9 (p. 81); V7♯11 on dominants resolving down a
  half step, down a fourth or up a whole step (p. 281).

**(b) V7 → minor defaults.**
- N&G p. 91 lists two entries: "V7(♭9): Mixolydian (♭9, ♯9, ♭13) (This is a composite of most minor key
  pitches.)" and "V7: Mixolydian (♭13)". The plain V7 keeps the natural 9; the ♭9 is the symbol's.
- M&H p. 95 and p. 98: V7 in minor = Mixolydian (♭9, ♯9, ♭13); "An alternative chord scale choice … is the altered
  dominant scale" (p. 95). Berklee therefore never prints the 7-note Phrygian Dominant for a primary V7 of
  minor; it prints the 8-note scale that contains it. (M&H do use the name "Phrygian dominant", for V7/III,
  p. 44 n. 12.)
- Levine's default is Altered: "a minor II-V-I usually consists of a half-diminished chord, an alt chord, and a
  minor-major chord" (p. 75); fn. 4 on p. 476 allows G7♭9 as well; G7♭9 = half-whole diminished (p. 81). On the
  harmonic minor: "you'd hear the great players playing it a lot, but they don't" (p. 76); it "is most often
  played over V7♭9 chords resolving to a minor chord a 5th below" and only in fragments (p. 476–477, with C and
  E♭ called "very dissonant" over G7♭9 on p. 478). On Mixolydian ♭6: "this mode is seldom played. Most jazz
  musicians, when they see the chord symbol G7♭13, improvise either on the altered scale or the whole-tone
  scale" (p. 66).
- Others: Chediak vol. 1 p. 344 gives V7(♭9) the harmonic minor scale from its fifth degree; p. 345 gives
  Mixolydian ♭13 "only in the preparation of IIm, where the ninth and ♭13 are diatonic (notes of the key)".
  Aebersold's Scale Syllabus menu lists "C7♭9 Spanish or Jewish scale" and "C7♭9 Diminished" as separate rows
  (see §5; web copy, no page numbers).
- Verdict: SPLIT, Berklee (8-note, altered optional) against Levine (altered or half-whole; harmonic minor as
  fragments). The engine's Phrygian Dominant is inside Berklee's set and equals Chediak's.

**(c) subV7 → Lydian Dominant: universal?** Yes as the default, in every primary: N&G p. 57; M&H p. 67 ("the
default chord scale for any substitute dominant chord, no matter what its target"); Levine pp. 64, 281 (V7♯11
resolving down a half step). Documented variants, none of which the engine needs for its Basic default: ♯9 for 9
on subV7 of a major target (N&G p. 58; M&H p. 74, 78–79); Dorian–Mixolydian when a related ii–V is used even for
a substitute (N&G p. 73); choose from the first or last chord of a chromatic series (N&G p. 64).

**(d) Back door ♭VII7.** Lydian ♭7: N&G p. 80 ("uses a Lydian♭7 scale most of the time"), p. 91; M&H p. 130
("the appropriate chord scale for ♭VII7 in a major key is Lydian ♭7"); Levine p. 281 (V7♯11 resolving "up a whole
step"), p. 283. The Mixolydian alternative is the "pure interchange" version (N&G p. 129; M&H p. 98 for ♭VII7 in
a minor key; M&H p. 238 "either"). Engine D5 = AGREE.

**(e) Extended dominants.** N&G p. 54 is explicit and matches D3. M&H and Levine name no scale (see table).

**(f) "V7 of the chord before."** No source treats the case; UNDERSPECIFIED.

**(g) 7sus4 and 7sus4(♭9); blues I7/IV7.** See the table rows s1–s3 and D5-blues.

## 2. Prose where a row needs it

**2.1 The ♯9 rider is the most consistent gap between engine and Berklee.** Everywhere the engine yields the 7-note
Phrygian Dominant (V7/iii, V7/vi, V7 → minor, V7/v in minor), both Berklee books give the 8-note scale. The
engine's §7.1 table says Spanish Phrygian is "only when the symbol or the chart asks." Berklee's text says the
opposite: the ♯9 is available whenever the ♭9 is (N&G 43). Principle 7 (diatonic at Basic, hipper at Advanced)
is a legitimate reason to print the 7-note scale at Basic. It should not be described as Berklee's scale.

**2.2 Plain `V7` to a minor chord.** N&G p. 91 prints "V7: Mixolydian (♭13)" and "V7(♭9)" as different lines.
For the unmarked symbol the engine derives ♭9 from natural minor (A♭ over G7 in C minor); N&G keep the natural 9
(A), which is the raised 6th of the composite minor. M&H (p. 95) put both in one scale. Levine: altered. No source
has the engine's 7-note scale for the plain symbol.

**2.3 The target-quality rule.** M&H p. 44 and p. 46 give a second, simpler rule: major 9 and 13 suggest a major
target; ♭9, ♯9, ♭13 suggest a minor target. It reproduces the engine's table for V7/IV, V7/V, V7/iii, V7/vi and V7
in major, and disagrees only on V7/ii (♯9 for 9) and on minor-key V7/iv (the full set for ♭13).

**2.3b Linear sources.** Ligon, Bergonzi and Crook were not available. Where the on-disk sources cut across
scale choice: (i) Levine p. 281 and N&G p. 57 agree that altered and Lydian Dominant are the same notes a
tritone apart (the sub and the V7alt resolve to the same chords), so a line over a subV7 cannot be told from a
line over its altered V7; (ii) Levine p. 282 ("Remember to alter V chords with caution during the melody … If the
melody note on a V chord is a natural 9th or 13th, an alt chord won't work … If the melody note is the ♭9, ♯9,
or ♭13, the ♯11 chord won't work") makes the melody, not the chord, the final arbiter; N&G p. 54 says the same
of sequential dominants ("The melody, or chord scale choice, or style, or composer's intention … The most
important consideration is sound"); (iii) N&G p. 114 and p. 127 treat an ascending diminished chord as the upper
structure of its V7(♭9) and give it the secondary-dominant scale; (iv) Levine p. 121–122: raising "every 'avoid'
note … the 11th on all unaltered dominant chords" is a practice exercise "to watch for opportunities to
reharmonize", not a default.

**2.4 The related ii–V of a substitute.** N&G p. 73 and M&H pp. 67–69, 74–75 disagree about the scale of the sub
itself when it follows its own ii. This is the only place where the two Berklee books point in different
directions on D2. The engine's Lydian Dominant is the safer Basic choice (M&H: "far and away the most widely used
expression of this function", p. 67).

## 3. Proposed changes

Principle 6 test applied to each reason string. Principle conflicts are stated and not resolved.

**Proposal 1. D5, IV7 of a blues: Mixolydian by default; Lydian Dominant stays in the menu.**
- Change: in a blues context only, IV7 (and the I7, unchanged) takes Mixolydian; the ♯11 is offered, not chosen.
- Reason string: "IV7 in a blues: a subdominant with dominant colour, not a V7 — Mixolydian (the ♯11 is an extra colour)".
- Test: chart `key: F`, row 5, `B♭7` in a 12-bar blues; expected scale B♭ Mixolydian, rule D5.
- Level: Basic (Mixolydian); Lydian Dominant is already in the `7` menu.
- Conflict: principle 4 (the key's ♯11) points the other way; §10.4 already names this exception ("rule D5 gets an
  exception for the blues context only"). Sources: N&G 105; M&H 93, 125, 136–137. Stop here: the lead decides.

**Proposal 2. s2: V7sus4 → plain Mixolydian; drop the automatic ♭6.**
- Change: remove the ♭13 test from s2; Mixolydian ♭6 stays in the `7sus4` menu (already listed "resolving to a minor chord").
- Reason string: "V7sus4 resolving down a fifth: Mixolydian, the 4th is a chord tone here, not an avoid note".
- Test: `G7sus4 | Cm7`, `key: C minor`; expected G Mixolydian, rule s2.
- Level: Basic. Sources: N&G 29, M&H 24, Levine 43; no source for the ♭6.

**Proposal 3. Ladder, not rule: V7 → minor Advanced alternative.**
- Change: add Altered as the Advanced alternative on the V7 → minor line (Levine's default, M&H's stated
  alternative); keep Spanish Phrygian as the Berklee 8-note option. D4 is unchanged.
- Reason string: unchanged ("V7 of G minor: ♭9 and ♭13 are in the key").
- Test: `Dm7♭5 | G7 | Cm7`, `key: C minor`; Basic: G Phrygian Dominant; Advanced: G Altered.
- Level: Advanced. Sources: Levine 75–76; M&H 95.

**Proposal 4. New rule D9 (gap): parallel dominants.**
- Change: three or more consecutive dominants that do not resolve down a fifth or a half step and move by one
  repeated interval take the same scale each, Mixolydian.
- Reason string: "parallel dominants: one sound moved along, Mixolydian on each".
- Test: a synthetic row `D7 | E7 | F7 | E7`, `key: G` (not a library chart); expected Mixolydian on all four.
- Level: Basic. Sources: N&G 123 ("each dominant chord will use the same chord scale as that of its parallel
  neighbor (usually Mixolydian)"); M&H 238 ("a non-resolving constant structure pattern", Mixolydian or Lydian dominant).

**Proposal 5. Documentation only (§10.2 and §10.6).** §10.2 should read "N&G's table says the same; M&H p. 45–46
prefer the ♯9/♭13 form and Levine p. 66 calls Mixolydian ♭6 seldom played." §10.6 should read "N&G p. 54 says the
same; M&H and Levine name no scale for extended dominants." No engine change.

## 4. Open questions the sources cannot settle

1. Whether a plain `V7` symbol in a minor key should print the natural 9 (N&G 91) or the ♭9 (engine, M&H 95).
2. The scale of a chain's head chord that resolves to a diatonic root with a changed quality (A7 → D7 in B♭):
   N&G 55 says V7/III; the engine says extended. N&G decide by harmonic stress, which the engine ignores (§14).
3. The "V7 of the chord before" case (§10.3): no source.
4. A ♭13 variant for a resolving sus: no source.
5. Blues I7: Mixolydian, Mixolydian ♯9, or the blues scale. N&G 105 and Levine 233 differ and the melody decides.
6. Minor-key D8 chains: N&G 54 literally says Mixolydian; M&H 99 supports derivation.
